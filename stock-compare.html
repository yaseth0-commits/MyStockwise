// What do these ratios mean?
P/E
Price-to-Earnings
How much you pay for every $1 the company earns. A P/E of 20 means you're paying $20 for each $1 of annual profit.
Lower = cheaper relative to earnings
PEG
Price/Earnings-to-Growth
P/E adjusted for how fast the company is growing. A PEG under 1 often signals the stock may be undervalued for its growth rate.
Under 1.0 is generally attractive
Div. Yield
Dividend Yield
The annual cash payout as a % of the stock price. Great for investors who want income, not just price appreciation.
Higher = more income per dollar
Payout
Payout Ratio
What % of earnings is paid out as dividends. Very high ratios (>80%) can be hard to sustain long-term if profits dip.
40–60% is often considered healthy
P/B
Price-to-Book
Compares market price to the company's net assets. Under 1 means the market values the company less than its book value.
Lower = closer to asset value
ROE
Return on Equity
How efficiently the company turns shareholder money into profit. Warren Buffett loves companies with consistently high ROE.
Above 15% is considered strong
